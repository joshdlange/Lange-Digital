If you are about to hire help with AI, here is how I would choose.

## A common bottleneck: hiring help without knowing what you are buying

Many owners end up with a tool installed and no change in how the work gets done. The partner was good at software and light on your business. Or the project was a program with a long timeline and no single thing you could point to afterward.

## Five questions to ask any partner

1. **What exact task does this take off our plate?** If the answer takes more than a sentence, keep your wallet closed.
2. **What does it cost, in writing, before work starts?** A fixed quote means the scope is understood. "We will see" means you carry the risk.
3. **Who owns it when the project ends?** The code, the data, the rules. You should.
4. **How do I get my data out, and what does it cost to switch?** Vague answers are the answer.
5. **How will we know it still works in six months?** Look for an owner, visible failures and a review habit.

A partner who can answer all five plainly is probably focused on a fix. A partner who dodges two or more is probably selling an install.

## How to spot the difference in the first call

Pay attention to what they ask you. A fix-focused partner asks about your week, where work piles up and what repeats. An install-focused partner asks which tools you want. The first conversation is about your bottleneck. The second is about their product.

## How it gets built to stay fixed

A good engagement looks small and concrete: find the one bottleneck, check what you already own, build only what is truly yours, make failures visible and hand over the pieces. Then, if it worked, pick the next one.
